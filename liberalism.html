Liberalism

Sub-theories: Democratic Peace Theory and Liberal Institutionalism

Liberalism is a school of thought in international relations that emphasizes the role of international law, institutions, and cooperation in promoting peace and stability. Liberalists believe that states can work together to achieve mutual benefits and that democratic governance and economic interdependence reduce the likelihood of conflict.

This is a theory that emphasizes cooperation, international institutions, economic, interdependence, and democracy to foster global peace, rejecting the realist view of inevitability of conflict. The strengths of democracy will strengthen peace alongside global institutions. Critiques of liberalism include that it is too idealistic and naive regarding power politics, and it is promoting a Western-centric order that often serves powerful states while ignoring system inequality.

Sub-theories

Democratic Peace Theory

Democratic Peace Theory states that democracies are more peaceful and therefore do not fight each other. An exampe of this would be the US justifying its invasion of Iraq, as they thought that by installing a democracy in Iraw, they would be able to lessen war, as democracies are more peaceful.

Liberal Institutionalism

Liberal institutionalism is the idea where insitutions spread information, which in turn reduces incentives to cheat in the international system. Cooperation is promoted through these reduced cheating incentives through orgnizations like the UN and NATO.

Does it work?

Liberalism has had some success in promoting peace and cooperation, particularly through the establishment of international institutions and agreements. However, it has also faced criticism for being too idealistic and for failing to account for power dynamics and inequalities in the international system.

Liberalism has both strengths and limitations in its approach to understanding international relations. With its emphasis on cooperation, institutions, and democracy, it provides a hopeful perspective on how the international system can be improved.
